## 1. Outcome & Scope

**Problem / context**
Cerberus today runs Codex, Gemini, and Claude in parallel and aggregates verdicts via priority-based consensus (P0/P1 = blocker, then majority/all/any over PASS). Each reviewer is unaware of peer outputs within a round; they only see prior-round reviews on outer-loop iteration after the artifact has been rewritten. The system thus captures **model diversity** but does not implement the higher-leverage primitives identified in `docs/debate.md` — anonymized peer broadcast, calibrated confidence-modulated belief updates, or strategy diversity. Reviewing artifacts is the canonical "judging task" type the design brief flags as the highest-value MAS use case, so Cerberus is exactly the right surface to land these primitives on.

**Goal**
Add an opt-in `--debate` mode to Cerberus reviews that injects one anonymized, confidence-tagged inner round of peer exchange between heterogeneous strategy-diverse reviewers, while preserving today's CLI surface, JSON schema, priority-based consensus, and Stop-hook integration.

**Success criteria**
- No formal numeric success metric in v1 — judged qualitatively ("on vibes") by the maintainer running it on real review artifacts and comparing debate vs. non-debate output side-by-side on at least three artifacts before the feature is flagged for general use.
- The single binary criterion that *is* enforced: when `--debate` is omitted from a non-help, non-error-on-rejected-flag invocation, every byte the user observes (CLI output, JSON outputs, `gate-state.json`, rendered reviewer prompts, Stop-hook flow) is identical to the pre-feature baseline. **Explicit exceptions** (these surfaces are part of the new feature and therefore allowed to differ from the pre-feature baseline): `--help` / usage output (must document the new `--debate` flag per R9), `--version` output if it shifts as part of the release, and error messages emitted for newly-rejected flag combinations (e.g., `--debate` passed to a non-judging `--type` per R10's bare-`spawn` whitelist). The byte-parity guarantee covers user-observed output of *valid invocations only*.
- **Falsifiable acceptance signal (primary v1 launch gate, in addition to byte-parity):** On a curated bad-artifact fixture (a spec checked into `bin/tests/fixtures/debate-bad-artifact/` containing a known P1 defect at a documented `file_path` + non-null `line_start`/`line_end` range), the test MUST assert **two conjunctive clauses, both required** (this is deliberately not a single ∀-quantified statement that could be vacuously satisfied if no Round-1 P1 exists at the planted location): **Clause 1 (existence in Round 1):** at least one reviewer MUST raise a P1 finding F at the planted defect's location in Round 1 — i.e., there exists a Round-1 reviewer output containing a finding F with `F.priority == "P1"`, non-null `F.file_path` exactly matching the planted defect's `file_path`, and non-null `F.line_start`/`F.line_end` whose integer range overlaps the planted defect's range per R6's integer line-range overlap predicate. If no such F exists in any reviewer's Round 1 output, the test FAILS — the fixture must be tuned (or reviewer prompts/strategies adjusted) until at least one reviewer raises the planted defect cold. **Clause 2 (retention in final-round aggregate):** that same defect MUST be retained as F' in `aggregate.json` of the final peer round, where F' satisfies the four pinned predicates: (i) `F'.priority == "P1"`, (ii) `F'.confidence >= 0.7`, (iii) `F'.file_path == F.file_path` (exact match, both non-null), and (iv) `F'.line_start`/`F'.line_end` are non-null and the range `[F'.line_start, F'.line_end]` overlaps `[F.line_start, F.line_end]` per R6's integer line-range overlap predicate (`F.line_start <= F'.line_end AND F'.line_start <= F.line_end`). Title is intentionally NOT required to match (different reviewers across rounds may rename the same defect); file location is. **Both clauses are required for the test to pass** — Clause 1 prevents vacuous satisfaction when no Round-1 P1 exists at the planted location; Clause 2 closes the no-op-Round-2 loophole (a no-op Round 2 that drops the Round-1 P1 about the planted defect but emits any unrelated P1 elsewhere with `confidence ≥ 0.7` MUST NOT pass — the file_path + line-overlap constraint pins the retention assertion to the known defect's location). Rationale: byte-parity proves the feature does no harm when off; this two-clause signal proves debate actually retains a real defect at its known location rather than washing it out — without it, an implementation could wire `--debate` to do nothing (e.g., a Round 2 prompt that ignores the peer block) and still pass the launch checklist, or could pass vacuously on a fixture where no reviewer raised the defect cold. Captured in the Launch checklist as a fixture-based test.
- **Additional vibes-checks (alternates, not gating):**
  - On a curated ambiguous-artifact fixture (an artifact where reviewers historically disagree), Round-2 `verdict` and `findings` are not 100% identical to Round-1 outputs across all reviewers — i.e., debate caused at least one reviewer to update their position.
  - On a curated `(peer abstained)` fixture (one reviewer set to always abstain), the surviving reviewers' Round-2 outputs reference the abstain in their reasoning OR remain stable from Round 1 — i.e., they don't fabricate phantom peer findings.
- Recorded in **Decisions Made** so future iterations don't have to re-derive this; v2 may revisit with a labelled fixture set or a sycophancy diagnostic.

**Non-goals**
- Replacing the existing parallel review pipeline outright — debate must coexist with the current single-pass behavior; `--debate` absent must be byte-for-byte equivalent to today for valid invocations (subject to the help / version / newly-rejected-flag-combination exceptions enumerated in Success criteria).
- Implementing the full set of 9 primitives from `docs/debate.md` in v1. Out of v1: sparsified communication (R4 / S²-MAD / CortexDebate), adaptive early exit (R5), label-free diagnostics including K\* / αK / disagreement-rate tracking (R7), adversarial robustness / Free-MAD trajectory scoring (R8), and cross-session safety memory (RedDebate).
- Building a general-purpose MAS framework outside Cerberus.
- Adding new reviewer providers beyond Codex / Gemini / Claude.
- Training or fine-tuning models — all primitives are prompt- and orchestration-level.
- Wiring debate into the *generator* commands (`create-spec`, `create-plan`, `create-tasks`, `healthcheck`, `architecture-review`). The design brief is explicit: debate's value lives in **judging**, not generation.
- Stylometric flattening of peer outputs — only deny-listed identity tokens are stripped (see R1).
- JSON-shape normalization of peer outputs in the anonymization pass.
- Per-agent confidence prior rescaling / Bayesian recalibration.
- New environment variables, including a `REVIEW_DEBATE_DISABLED` kill-switch. Rollback is "drop the flag from invocations" or revert the plugin version.

**Constraints**
- **Must extend `bin/review-gate`** — debate is a new flag and behavior path inside the existing gate, not a new binary. The state file (`gate-state.json`), reviews directory, telemetry, and Stop-hook integration are reused as-is.
- **Must remain compatible with the JSON verdict schema** — `findings`, `verdict`, `summary` MUST continue to be produced exactly as today; new fields (`confidence`, `strategy`, `round`, `peer_responses_seen`) are additive only and absent in non-debate runs. The structured-output schema fed to Codex (today written to `$REVIEWS_DIR/review-schema.json` by `bin/review-gate`, with `additionalProperties: false` at both the top level and inside `findings[*]`) MUST remain byte-identical for non-debate runs; the new fields require a debate-conditional schema variant per R9 because Codex's `--output-schema` will otherwise reject or strip them.
- **Must work with the existing reviewer CLIs** (`codex`, `gemini`, `claude`) — no new SDK or service dependencies. New behavior is implemented purely via prompt template changes and orchestration in `bin/review-gate`.
- **Must remain Bash 3.2 compatible** (macOS shell) — no associative arrays or Bash-4-isms.
- **Must respect Gemini read-only policy** (`config/gemini-readonly-policy.toml`) — debate prompts and peer broadcasts must not coax Gemini into write tools, and the policy must continue to apply on every Gemini call under debate.
- **Must preserve consensus modes** (`majority` / `all` / `any`) — confidence is used for tiebreaking and finding deduplication only; the existing P0/P1-blocking rule is unchanged.
- **Must reuse intelligence-mode plumbing** — `--mode fast / smart / max` continue to drive model selection. Debate is orthogonal: it inherits mode, adds rounds.
- **Must hard-error if fewer than 2 reviewers are available when `--debate` is set** — debate has no useful single-agent fallback, so silent degradation is forbidden; users must know debate didn't happen.
- **Plugin distribution:** All new assets live under the plugin root (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`) so a plugin reinstall is sufficient to pick up changes.
- **Backwards compatibility:** Every existing CI invocation that does not pass `--debate` and is a *valid invocation* (i.e., not `--help`, not a rejected flag combination introduced by this feature) MUST behave byte-for-byte identically to the prior version. No changes to default mode, default rounds, default consensus, or default JSON shape. The explicit exceptions enumerated in Success criteria (help output, version output, and error messages for newly-rejected flag combinations) are part of the new feature surface and are permitted to differ.
- **Bare-`spawn` debate whitelist:** Bare `bin/review-gate spawn` accepts `--debate` only when `--type` is one of `code`, `plan`, `spec`, or `epic-verify` (these are the existing internal `REVIEW_TYPE` values used by `bin/review-gate`, by the prompt templates under `prompts/reviewers/<type>.md`, by the revision templates under `prompts/revisions/<type>.md`, by the reviewer-output frontmatter, and by the `mode.type` field in `gate-state.json`; the four named subcommands `spawn-code-review`, `spawn-plan-review`, `spawn-spec-review`, `spawn-epic-verify` are CLI sugar that set `--type` to the corresponding short name). Any other `--type` value (including `healthcheck`, `architecture-review`, `create-tasks`, `manual`, `auto`, or any auto-detected type that doesn't match the four judging types) MUST cause `bin/review-gate` preflight to reject `--debate` with a clear error before any model is invoked. The four `spawn-*-review` named subcommands implicitly carry the right `--type` and so always accept `--debate`. Full requirement and verification in R10.

**Scope boundary**
This spec covers (a) adding `--debate` flag plumbing through `spawn`, `spawn-code-review`, `spawn-plan-review`, `spawn-spec-review`, `spawn-epic-verify`; (b) prompt-template changes per reviewer to elicit confidence + accept a strategy directive + consume peer broadcasts; (c) anonymization of peer outputs between rounds; (d) confidence-weighted aggregation that respects the existing P0/P1 priority gate; (e) hard-error preflight for fewer than 2 reviewers under `--debate`. It does NOT cover instrumentation/diagnostics beyond what's already in the iteration telemetry, sparsification, K\* tracking, adaptive early exit, cross-session memory, adversarial detection, or any change to non-judging commands.

**Alternatives considered**
- **Env-var rollout knob (`REVIEW_DEBATE_DISABLED`).** Rejected. The opt-in flag already gives a one-step rollback ("drop `--debate`"); a kill-switch env var would expand the matrix of states the v1 gate has to support without a corresponding rollback story improvement. Reverting the plugin version remains the heavyweight rollback path.
- **Quantitative success metric (sycophancy diagnostic, disagreement-rate plateau, K\* ratio).** Rejected for v1. Each requires a labelled fixture set or a calibrated embedding model to be load-bearing, and v1 would have to ship them before the feature itself could ship. Recording "no metric" deliberately so v2 owns the diagnostic question, not v1.
- **Stylometric flattening (LLM-rewrite of peer outputs to neutralize voice).** Rejected. It costs another model call per peer per round, and the maintainer chose to accept residual stylistic leakage on the explicit reasoning that Round 2 still captures the cross-model-evidence win even if reviewers occasionally guess "that sounded like Codex."
- **Per-agent prior rescaling of confidence.** Rejected for v1. Requires a labelled validation set to learn priors, which doesn't exist. Self-reported confidence with shared anchors is the smallest credible step.
- **Sparsified peer broadcast (S²-MAD-style filter).** Deferred to v2. With only 3 reviewers and 2 rounds, the token cost of full broadcast is bounded enough to pay; the complexity of similarity-thresholded routing is not warranted.

## 2. User Experience & Flows

**UX impact** — Cerberus is a CLI/plugin, so "users" are engineers running `/cerberus:*` commands and the CI/automation that wraps them.
- New flag `--debate` on `spawn` and the four `spawn-*-review` commands.
- Help output and `README.md` updated with a "Debate mode" section explaining the flag, the round shape, and the `<2 reviewers` hard-error.
- The Stop-hook gate report contains an additional one-line "Debate: round X/Y" indicator when debate ran; the existing reviewer table gains a `Strategy` column when debate ran. JSON output for non-debate runs is unchanged.
- Token usage per run is roughly 2× a non-debate run on the same artifact under `fast` / `smart` (Round 1 + Round 2 over the same N reviewers), and roughly 3× under `max` (Round 1 + 2 anonymized peer rounds). This is documented but not actively budgeted in v1.

**Primary flow**
1. User invokes a review command with the flag, e.g. `/cerberus:review-code --debate` or `/cerberus:review-spec docs/foo.md --debate`.
2. `review-gate` resolves agents, mode, max gate iterations, and **debate-on**.
3. **Preflight:** if fewer than 2 of the requested reviewers are available, exit non-zero with a clear error before any model is invoked. The error names the constraint and the resolved agent set.
4. **Strategy assignment:** for each active reviewer R in canonical alphabetical order (`claude`, `codex`, `gemini`), compute `shasum -a 256` over `<artifact_id> ":" <R>`, take the first 8 hex chars of the digest, parse as base-16, and reduce modulo 3 to get R's preferred index into the fixed array `[verification-first, falsification-first, decompose]`. If the preferred index is already taken by an earlier reviewer in canonical order, walk forward `(index+1) mod 3, (index+2) mod 3` until a free strategy is found. Same artifact + same reviewer → same strategy on rerun (per-(artifact, reviewer) stability); swapping one reviewer changes only that reviewer's slot directly, plus any deterministic collision-driven shift; different artifact → shuffled. The `<artifact_id>` value is pinned per review type (plan/spec → realpath of the source file; code → `diff_args_str`; epic-verify → realpath of the epic file or verbatim raw-criteria string); full requirement and edge cases in R3.
5. **Round 1 (initial / cold):** all reviewers receive the artifact + reviewer template + a strategy directive. Each emits the existing `findings` / `verdict` / `summary` plus per-finding `confidence ∈ [0,1]` and an `overall_confidence ∈ [0,1]`. Calibration anchor language is included in every prompt verbatim. Reviewers are NOT shown peer outputs in Round 1.
6. **Anonymization layer** between rounds strips identity tokens from each peer's Round-1 output (see R1) and randomizes peer ordering per recipient. Each peer is assigned an opaque, run-local ID (e.g., `Peer-A`, `Peer-B`). No stylometric rewriting.
7. **Round 2 (debate):** each reviewer receives the artifact, the same strategy directive, the **anonymized peer block** (each peer's `findings` (`title` + `body`), `verdict`, and `overall_confidence`; per-finding confidence and per-peer `summary` are NOT included in the displayed block — kept in JSON for telemetry), plus a **prior-round-self block** containing reviewer R's own Round 1 output (its `verdict`, `findings` with per-finding `confidence`, and `overall_confidence` — same fields the JSON contains). Because reviewer CLIs are stateless one-shot invocations, this prior-round-self block is what gives R memory of its own prior position; without it R cannot purposefully yield or resist based on its prior confidence. The prior-round-self block is NOT subject to the R1 anonymization deny-list (it is the reviewer's own labelled output, presented back to itself), and the prompt MUST clearly mark this block as "yours" and the peer block as "peers" so the reviewer cannot confuse the two. Under `--mode max`, Round 3's prior-round-self block contains R's Round 2 output (most recent round only — never a concatenation of all prior rounds), paired with the Round 2 anonymized peer block. Reviewers re-emit findings/verdict/confidence, conditioned on peer confidence per the prompt template (high-confidence peer dissent should pull a low-confidence prior; high-confidence prior should resist sycophantic peer pressure).
8. **Aggregator** consumes the final peer round's outputs (Round 2 under `fast`/`smart`, Round 3 under `max`) and produces two artifacts:
    - **Per-reviewer review JSONs**, one per active reviewer, each containing that reviewer's final-round `findings` / `verdict` / `summary` exactly as that reviewer produced them (with the additive debate fields). These are the per-reviewer artifacts written into the reviews directory and are byte-shape-compatible with today's reviewer outputs — the Stop-hook reads them like it does today.
    - **`aggregate.json`**, a cross-reviewer merged view containing the deduplicated `findings` array (near-duplicate findings raised by multiple reviewers are merged with the highest-confidence body retained as primary and peer bodies attached as supporting views), a confidence-weighted `summary`, and an aggregate `verdict` that applies confidence as a tiebreaker for PASS / NEEDS_WORK splits under the configured consensus mode. Confidence is used in exactly two places — cross-reviewer near-duplicate finding merge and verdict tiebreaks — and never to suppress a P0/P1 finding.
9. **Source-of-truth split.** The Stop-hook continues to run its existing priority-then-consensus calculator over the per-reviewer review JSONs unchanged: any P0/P1 finding from any reviewer triggers `requires_decision`, then the configured `majority` / `all` / `any` consensus is applied to the per-reviewer Round-2 (or Round-3 under `max`) verdicts. The gate report displays the `aggregate.json` cross-reviewer-merged finding set (one card per defect) plus the aggregate verdict for human inspection. If the Stop-hook's per-reviewer consensus and the `aggregate.json` verdict disagree (possible only on a 2-reviewer split where the aggregate's confidence tiebreak picks the lower-vote-count side), the **Stop-hook's per-reviewer consensus is authoritative for gate pass/fail**; the aggregate verdict is reported alongside as additional context. There is one decision authority — the existing Stop-hook calculator over per-reviewer verdicts — and one cross-reviewer presentation surface — `aggregate.json`.
10. **Outer-loop iteration (existing behavior):** if FAIL, the artifact is rewritten by Claude and the gate iterates. The next iteration starts a **fresh debate** — new Round 1, no carry-over of prior debate transcript. The existing "previous reviews" block in the iteration prompt continues to work as today (it surfaces the prior iteration's final aggregated findings, not the round-by-round debate trail).

**Coordinator (inner-round driver)**

Today's `bin/review-gate spawn` (and the four `spawn-*-review` variants) fire reviewers off in parallel and the Stop-hook waits on the reviews directory. Under `--debate`, that single async fire-and-forget step becomes a **synchronous in-process coordinator** owned by `spawn` itself. The coordinator:

1. Runs strategy assignment and preflight (already complete by step 4 above).
2. Launches all Round 1 reviewers in parallel and waits for all to complete (or to be marked `abstained` on per-reviewer timeout / crash, per the existing failure handling).
3. Runs the anonymization pass in-process over the collected Round 1 outputs (no extra model call, deny-list scrub + opaque ID assignment + per-recipient peer ordering). **Eligibility check before launching Round 2:** if fewer than 2 reviewers are non-abstained after Round 1 (i.e., it has become mathematically impossible for the active final-round count to reach 2), the coordinator hard-errors per the degraded-below-2 trigger in R6 before launching any Round 2 model and exits without populating the reviews directory.
4. Launches Round 2 reviewers in parallel — **only the reviewers that did NOT abstain in Round 1** (per the terminal-abstention rule above; an abstained Round-1 reviewer is NOT re-invoked in Round 2). The anonymized peer block presented to each active Round-2 reviewer still surfaces every Round-1 peer slot, including abstained Round-1 peers as `(peer abstained)` per R1. Waits for all launched Round-2 reviewers to complete or abstain.
5. Under `--mode max` only: **eligibility check before launching Round 3** — if fewer than 2 reviewers are non-abstained across all prior rounds (Round 1 AND Round 2), the coordinator hard-errors per the degraded-below-2 trigger in R6 before launching any Round 3 model. Otherwise, re-runs the anonymization pass over Round 2 outputs and launches Round 3 reviewers — **only the reviewers that have been non-abstained across ALL prior rounds (Round 1 AND Round 2)**; any reviewer that abstained in Round 1 or Round 2 is NOT invoked in Round 3 (terminal-abstention rule). The anonymized peer block presented to each active Round-3 reviewer still surfaces every Round-2 peer slot, including abstained Round-2 peers (and Round-1-abstained reviewers, who carry the abstention forward) as `(peer abstained)` per R1. Waits for all launched Round-3 reviewers to complete or abstain.
6. Runs the aggregator in-process over the final peer round's outputs.
7. **Only after aggregation completes**, writes the per-reviewer final-round review JSONs into the reviews directory and writes `aggregate.json` alongside them. Following the existing convention that `bin/review-gate wait` and the Stop-hook poll for, the coordinator also writes a `<reviewer>.done` sentinel file alongside each per-reviewer review JSON for every reviewer active in the final peer round (i.e., the same set listed in `aggregate.json.reviewers[]`); abstained-final-round reviewers under Option B have no per-reviewer JSON written and therefore no `.done` or `.failed` sentinel — their slot is absent from the reviews directory entirely. The aggregator-failure and SIGINT-cancellation paths write neither per-reviewer JSONs nor sentinels (the on-disk state is empty per the Cancellation / Aggregator-failure subsections below), so `bin/review-gate wait --json` against those paths sees no sentinels and returns `status: "timeout"` once `--timeout` elapses.

Only after step 7 does control return such that the Stop-hook sees the reviews directory populated. The Stop-hook then operates exactly as today — reads the reviews directory, calculates priority-then-consensus over the per-reviewer verdicts, surfaces the gate report (extended with the `aggregate.json` cross-reviewer view per step 9 and the debate indicators per the UX impact list).

**Cancellation.** A SIGINT received during Round 1 or Round 2 (or Round 3 under `max`) lets the in-flight reviewers in the current round complete (or hit their per-reviewer timeout); the coordinator then writes whatever rounds completed as partial telemetry into the per-iteration directory and exits without populating the reviews directory. Concretely: `gate-state.json.status` remains `"pending"` (the value `spawn` wrote when it created the state file — the coordinator does NOT flip status to `"awaiting_decision"` on cancel because the Stop-hook's consensus path, which is what writes `"awaiting_decision"`, never runs without a populated reviews directory). No new debate-specific field is added to `gate-state.json` to mark in-progress debate state; partial debate progress lives only in the per-iteration telemetry directory (the same `iterations/<iter>/` path used for per-round telemetry — the cancelled run's directory contains whatever round telemetry completed before the SIGINT and is never consumed by any subsequent decision surface). `bin/review-gate wait --json` invoked against the cancelled gate behaves exactly as today against an empty reviews directory: it polls until per-reviewer `.done`/`.failed` sentinels appear, and since none ever will, it returns `status: "timeout"` with exit code 3 once `--timeout` elapses (or `status: "no_reviewers"` exit code 4 if the reviewers map in `gate-state.json` is empty). No partial `aggregate.json` is written; partial debate state never reaches the Stop-hook's consensus calculator.

**Aggregator failure.** If the in-process aggregator itself crashes between step 6 and step 7 above (e.g., a `jq` parse error on a malformed reviewer JSON, an unhandled shell error, an OOM, or any non-zero exit from the aggregator code path), the on-disk shape mirrors the Cancellation case exactly: the reviews directory is NOT populated, no per-reviewer final-round review JSONs are written, no `aggregate.json` is written, and `gate-state.json.status` stays at `"pending"` (the coordinator does NOT flip status to `"awaiting_decision"` for the same reason as Cancellation — the Stop-hook's consensus path never runs without a populated reviews directory). Partial round telemetry — raw reviewer JSONs from rounds that completed before aggregation was attempted — is preserved in the per-iteration telemetry directory (`iterations/<iter>/`) for inspection, exactly as in the Cancellation case. **Distinguishability from cancellation/hang (required):** the coordinator MUST emit a distinct stderr error message of the form `aggregator failed: <reason>` (e.g., `aggregator failed: jq parse error on reviews/codex.json`) and MUST exit with a distinct non-zero exit code reserved for this failure mode (separate from the SIGINT-cancelled exit and from per-reviewer timeout exits) so users and CI can tell an aggregator crash apart from a hang or a SIGINT cancel without inspecting the telemetry directory. The exact reserved exit code is fixed at implementation time; what is pinned here is that it is non-zero, distinct from the SIGINT and timeout codes, and stable across runs. **Recovery is identical to Cancellation:** re-run `spawn` with `--debate` (the active-gate guard rejects this for 30 min unless `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is set; `bin/review-gate resolve` is optional cleanup). The coordinator MUST NOT swallow the aggregator crash or produce a UX identical to a hung debate — silent failure here would be indistinguishable from cancellation on disk, and the stderr marker plus the reserved exit code is the only signal that tells users which path occurred.

**Resume.** v1 has no mid-debate resume. Recovery is "re-run `spawn` with `--debate`": the existing active-gate guard (`bin/review-gate` rejects a fresh spawn against a `pending` or `awaiting_decision` gate younger than 30 min unless `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is set) governs whether the re-spawn lands directly or requires the rerun override; either way the new run starts a fresh Round 1 with a freshly-written `gate-state.json` for the new iteration and overwrites `reviews/` and any debate telemetry under the new iteration's directory. `bin/review-gate resolve` is **optional** for recovery — it is not required to re-spawn under `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1`, but invoking it (with or without `--reason`) cleanly clears the cancelled gate to `status: "resolved"` / `decision.action = "proceed"` so the next `spawn` does not collide with the active-gate guard at all. Any partial telemetry from the prior cancelled run is preserved on disk for inspection under the cancelled iteration's directory (`iterations/<cancelled-iter>/`) but is not consumed by the next run, the Stop-hook, or the aggregator. (Outer-loop iteration semantics are unchanged.)

**Key states**
- **Empty state:** No prior reviews, debate not yet run; command initiates Round 1. Special sub-case: if every reviewer returns PASS with empty findings, the gate aggregates to PASS and the gate report still shows the "Debate: N rounds" banner plus the Strategy column for traceability.
- **In-progress state:** Round 1 collected, Round 2 prompts being built or running. Per-round progress is surfaced with phases labelled (Round 1 → anonymizing → Round 2 → aggregating). Telemetry captures wall-clock per phase.
- **Reviewer abstain (Round k):** A reviewer crashed, timed out, or returned unparseable JSON — that reviewer's contribution to round k is recorded as `abstained=true`. Their findings, verdict, and confidence are absent for that round and contribute nothing to aggregation **for that round** (no 0.0 or 0.5 placeholder enters the confidence math). **Abstention is terminal for the run: a reviewer that abstains in round k is NOT invoked in any subsequent round (k+1, k+2, ...) — it remains abstained for the rest of the run.** Rationale: a Round-1 crash/timeout is a strong signal the reviewer is unhealthy for this run; retrying it in Round 2 risks duplicating the failure mode and wasting time/tokens, and treating abstention as terminal avoids partial-state edge cases (e.g., a reviewer that abstained in Round 1 succeeds in Round 2 — what's its overall_confidence? does the Round-2 prompt include a self-block referencing absent Round-1 output?). The abstained reviewer's earlier-round output is preserved in telemetry only and is NOT carried forward into the final aggregation, the per-reviewer review JSONs written for the Stop-hook, or `aggregate.json` (Option B — see the "Reviewer crash mid-round" alternate flow and R6). The abstained reviewer's slot still surfaces to active peers in subsequent rounds as `(peer abstained)` per R1, preserving the round-shape signal for active peers. Debate continues if ≥2 reviewers remain non-abstained in the **final peer round**. (Distinct from a reviewer that produced parseable JSON but is missing required fields — see the "Confidence not produced by a reviewer" alternate flow and R2 edge cases.)
- **Final state:** the final peer round (Round 2 under `fast`/`smart`, Round 3 under `max`) is complete, the aggregator has produced the per-reviewer review JSONs and `aggregate.json`; gate proceeds with the standard consensus + Stop-hook flow over the per-reviewer JSONs.
- **Degraded state (preflight):** Fewer than 2 reviewers available + `--debate` → hard error before any model is called.
- **Outer-loop iterating:** Existing behavior. Gate's own `--max-rounds` controls outer iterations; debate's two inner rounds run inside each.

**Alternate flows**
- **User does not pass `--debate`** — existing single-pass behavior unchanged. No anonymization, no confidence requirement, no extra rounds, no JSON additions.
- **`--debate` with `--mode fast` or `--mode smart`** — 2 inner rounds total (Round 1 cold + Round 2 peer).
- **`--debate` with `--mode max`** — 3 inner rounds total (Round 1 cold + 2 anonymized peer rounds). The third round receives the previous round's anonymized peer blocks. `ultrathink` prefix and `max` model selection are unchanged.
- **`--debate` with `--consensus all` or `--consensus any`** — aggregator's verdict computation respects the chosen mode's semantics; confidence is a tiebreaker, not an override.
- **User cancels mid-debate** (Ctrl-C / kill) — partial round outputs are persisted to the per-iteration telemetry directory (`iterations/<iter>/`). The reviews directory is NOT populated. `gate-state.json.status` stays at `"pending"` (the value `spawn` wrote on entry; the coordinator does not flip it to `"awaiting_decision"` on cancel — that transition is owned by the Stop-hook's consensus path, which never runs because there are no per-reviewer review JSONs to consume). No debate-specific in-progress marker field is added to `gate-state.json`. `bin/review-gate wait --json` against the cancelled gate returns `status: "timeout"` (exit 3) after `--timeout` elapses, or `status: "no_reviewers"` (exit 4) if no reviewers were registered before SIGINT. Recovery: re-run `spawn` with `--debate` (the active-gate guard rejects this for 30 min unless `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is set on the re-spawn). `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/bin/review-gate resolve` is optional for cleanup — it flips the gate to `resolved`/`proceed` so the next `spawn` does not collide with the active-gate guard, but is not required for recovery if `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is used. Partial telemetry from the cancelled run is preserved on disk for inspection but is never consumed by the next run, the Stop-hook, or the aggregator.
- **Reviewer crash mid-round** (Mode A — crash, timeout, or unparseable JSON) — that reviewer's round-k slot is `abstained=true`; their findings, verdict, and confidence are absent and contribute nothing to aggregation for that round (no 0.0 or 0.5 placeholder enters the confidence math). **Abstention is terminal for the run (pinned eligibility rule, applied uniformly across Section 2, R1, R6, and Decisions Made):** a reviewer that abstains in round k is NOT invoked in any subsequent round k+1, k+2, ... — it remains abstained for the rest of the run. The coordinator's Round 2 launch (step 4 above) launches only Round-1 non-abstained reviewers; the coordinator's Round 3 launch under `--mode max` (step 5 above) launches only reviewers non-abstained across all prior rounds. The rejected alternative (re-invoke an abstained reviewer in a later round, hoping it recovers) was considered and rejected because (a) a Round-1 crash/timeout is a strong signal the reviewer is unhealthy for this run, and retrying duplicates the failure mode while consuming time and tokens, and (b) it creates partial-state edge cases (e.g., a Round-1-abstained reviewer that succeeds in Round 2 — what's its overall_confidence? does the Round-2 prompt include a self-block referencing absent Round-1 output?). If a reviewer abstains in Round 1, their slot still surfaces to Round 2 peers as `(peer abstained)` in the anonymized peer block (informative absence rather than silent omission — see R1 edge cases) so peers do not miscount the round's information shape; the same applies to Round-2 abstainers under `--mode max` (their slot surfaces to Round-3 peers as `(peer abstained)`), and to Round-1 abstainers under `--mode max` (the abstention propagates forward — Round-1 abstainers also surface to Round-3 peers as `(peer abstained)`). **If a reviewer abstains in the final peer round (Round 2 under `fast`/`smart`, Round 3 under `max`), that reviewer is fully excluded from the final aggregation** — no per-reviewer review JSON is written for them into the reviews directory, they do NOT contribute to `aggregate.json`'s `findings`, `verdict`, or `summary`, and the Stop-hook's per-reviewer consensus calculator does NOT see them (Option B — exclude from final aggregation, chosen over carry-forward because it avoids mixing rounds in the canonical outputs and matches R6's "consumes the final peer round" framing). Their earlier-round output is preserved in the per-iteration telemetry directory for inspection but is not consumed by any decision surface. **Active-reviewer count for the degraded-below-2 gate** is the count of reviewers with a parseable, non-abstained output in the **final peer round**; if that count is fewer than 2 once the final peer round resolves (or it becomes mathematically impossible for it to reach 2 mid-debate, e.g., abstentions in an earlier round leave fewer than 2 reviewers eligible — i.e., non-abstained — to attempt the final round under the terminal-abstention rule), the run errors out and the gate goes to `requires_decision` with a clear error reason ("debate degraded below 2 active reviewers in the final peer round"). Concrete examples: with 2 reviewers and 1 abstaining in Round 1, Round 2 cannot launch (only 1 eligible reviewer) → degraded-below-2 hard error. With 3 reviewers and 1 abstaining in Round 1, Round 2 launches with the 2 remaining non-abstained reviewers; the abstained Round-1 reviewer is NOT re-invoked. With 3 reviewers under `--mode max` and 1 abstaining in Round 1 plus 1 different reviewer abstaining in Round 2, Round 3 cannot launch (only 1 reviewer non-abstained across all prior rounds) → degraded-below-2 hard error.
- **Confidence not produced by a reviewer** (Mode B — parseable JSON but missing or out-of-range confidence fields) — if the reviewer's JSON parses and contains both `findings` and `verdict` but `overall_confidence` is absent, it is treated as `0.5` (uncertain default — distinguishes "didn't answer" from "had nothing to say"); a missing per-finding `confidence` is also treated as `0.5`; out-of-range values (e.g., `2.0`, `-0.3`) are clamped to `[0,1]`; an integrity warning is logged. The reviewer is NOT abstained — their findings still count for aggregation. **Mode B applies ONLY to the confidence fields** (`overall_confidence` and per-finding `confidence`), since confidence is a debate-only addition with a sensible default. **Missing `findings` (the array itself absent) or missing `verdict` is Mode A (abstain entirely for that round)**, not Mode B, because `findings` and `verdict` are the existing core schema that must be present for the reviewer to be considered to have produced output at all — there is no content to count, so substituting defaults (empty findings, default verdict) is rejected. **Distinction:** `findings: []` (empty array — the reviewer saw no defects) IS valid output and counts toward the active-reviewer count; `findings` absent (the field itself missing from the parsed JSON) is Mode A. This is also distinct from Mode A's other triggers (crash / timeout / unparseable JSON), where the reviewer abstains for the round entirely and contributes no confidence value at all (see "Reviewer crash mid-round" above and R2 edge cases). Defense-in-depth note: R9's strict `additionalProperties: false` schema with `findings`/`verdict` as required properties should already reject any reviewer JSON missing these fields at schema-validation time; this Mode A boundary defines the runtime semantics if such a JSON ever reaches the aggregator (e.g., post-repair-path output, or a future schema relaxation).
- **Reviewer count exceeds 3** — per-(artifact, reviewer) hashing assigns each reviewer a preferred index; collision-resolution walk wraps so some reviewers share a strategy deterministically. The gate report shows the actual assignment.
- **Reviewer count exactly 2** — each reviewer's preferred index is computed independently; canonical-order collision resolution guarantees the two reviewers receive distinct strategies; debate proceeds with two peer blocks. The unused third strategy in the array is simply not assigned.
- **Generator commands invoked with `--debate`** — at the slash-command wrapper layer in v1, the wrapper does NOT validate `--debate` placement (per R10's rejection-layer commitment); the flag is either silently ignored by the wrapper or passed through to `bin/generate` which fails on the unknown flag. Bare `bin/review-gate spawn --type <generator-type> --debate` (e.g., `--type healthcheck`, `--type architecture-review`, `--type create-tasks`) IS rejected at `bin/review-gate` preflight with a clear error before any model is invoked. Wrapper-layer rejection in slash-command markdown is a v2 polish item.
- **Bare `spawn` with non-judging `--type` and `--debate`** — `bin/review-gate spawn` rejects `--debate` at preflight whenever `--type` is not one of `code`, `plan`, `spec`, `epic-verify` (the existing internal `REVIEW_TYPE` values). The error message names the rejected `--type` value and the four allowed values. No model is invoked. See R10.
- **Outer-loop revision** — when Claude rewrites the artifact and the gate re-runs, debate restarts from a fresh Round 1 with no carry-over of prior anonymized transcripts.

## 3. Requirements + Verification

**R1 — Anonymized peer broadcast** *(Core)*
- **Requirement:** When `--debate` is set and Round 2 (or, under `--mode max`, Round 3) runs, the system MUST present each reviewer with peer outputs from the previous round under opaque per-run IDs only (e.g., `Peer-A`, `Peer-B`). The displayed peer block MUST have all agent-identity tokens removed and the order of peer entries randomized per recipient. Anonymization MUST use a deny-list approach: a configurable regex set covering at minimum `{Claude, Codex, Gemini, GPT, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Agent 1, Agent 2, Agent 3}` matched case-insensitive and whole-word. **Pinned regex flavor:** POSIX Extended Regular Expressions (ERE) as implemented by `grep -oiE` (already the flavor used elsewhere in `bin/review-gate`); the implementation MUST scrub via `grep -oiE` (or an equivalent POSIX ERE engine — `sed -E`, `awk`'s ERE, jq's `gsub` with `"i"` flag) so the word-boundary semantics are stable across macOS Bash 3.2 (BSD grep) and Linux (GNU grep). **Whole-word semantics under POSIX ERE:** a "word character" is `[A-Za-z0-9_]`; `-`, `/`, `.`, `:`, and whitespace are NOT word characters and therefore ARE word boundaries. **Canonical word-boundary form (pinned for portability):** each deny-list term MUST be wrapped with the explicit non-word-character class form `(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])<term>($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])`, and the alternation MUST be applied with the `-i` (case-insensitive) flag. The BSD-only `[[:<:]]` / `[[:>:]]` POSIX bracket-class boundaries and the GNU-only `\<` / `\>` / `\b` boundaries MUST NOT be used: GNU grep does not recognize `[[:<:]]` / `[[:>:]]` under `-E`, and BSD grep does not recognize `\<` / `\>` / `\b`, so either platform-specific form would silently fail on the other platform before any anonymization runs. **Implementation pattern:** the leading and trailing non-word characters MUST be captured as groups (e.g., `(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])(<term>)($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])`) so a `sed -E`-style replacement can substitute the matched deny-list token with a redaction placeholder while preserving the surrounding boundary characters in the scrubbed text — equivalently, after a `grep -oiE` match the leading/trailing non-word characters MUST be stripped from the matched span before the redaction is applied. Note that under this canonical form the matched span includes one boundary character on each side; implementations MUST NOT redact those boundary characters. **Adjacent-term iteration (pinned).** Because the canonical pattern's negated character classes explicitly consume one boundary character on each side, a single non-iterative `sed -E` (or equivalent) pass over a buffer containing two adjacent deny-list terms separated by exactly one boundary character (e.g., `Claude Codex`) will leave the second term unredacted: the first match consumes the shared space as its trailing boundary, leaving the next term's `C` with no preceding non-word character available for the next match in the same pass. The substitution MUST therefore (a) preserve the captured boundary characters by emitting them in the replacement (canonical form: `s/(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])<term>($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])/\1[REDACTED]\2/gi` — the literal `\1` and `\2` re-emit the captured leading and trailing boundary so the next pass sees an intact boundary on the now-adjacent next term), AND (b) be run iteratively until the buffer is idempotent (no further substitutions occur). **Canonical pinned loop form (portable do-while shell loop, works under macOS Bash 3.2 / BSD sed and Linux / GNU sed):** read the input into a variable `cur`, then repeat `next=$(printf '%s' "$cur" | sed -E "s/(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])(<term1>|<term2>|...)($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])/\1[REDACTED]\3/gi"); [ "$next" = "$cur" ] && break; cur=$next` until the loop breaks; the final `cur` is the scrubbed output. (The deny-list alternation inserts an extra capture group, so the trailing boundary is `\3` rather than `\2`; implementations MUST count capture groups consistently with whatever alternation form they emit. The single-term illustration above uses `\1[REDACTED]\2` because it has only the two boundary captures.) The `sed -E` BSD-label form `:a; s/.../.../gi; ta` MAY be used as an equivalent in-process loop, but the do-while shell loop is the pinned canonical form because it is unambiguously portable across the BSD and GNU `sed` dialects without per-platform `\`-newline handling between commands. The peer block MUST contain, for each peer, exactly that peer's `findings` (`title` + `body`), `verdict`, and `overall_confidence`, and MUST NOT contain that peer's per-finding `confidence` or per-peer `summary`. The system MUST NOT attempt stylometric flattening (no LLM rewrite of peer output) and MUST NOT perform JSON-shape normalization in v1. **Per-peer rendered shape (pinned).** Each peer entry within the peer block MUST be rendered text (markdown), not raw JSON, and MUST follow one of two canonical skeletons depending on whether the peer is active or abstained. The two skeletons are paired and exhaustive — every peer entry is exactly one of these two shapes. Line endings are LF only with no trailing whitespace; `\n` below denotes a literal LF byte. Surrounding peer-block glue (a section header introducing the peer block, separators between peer entries) MAY differ across the four review-type templates, but each peer entry's shape is fixed and byte-identical across templates. **Active-peer skeleton** (used when the peer produced a parseable, non-abstained prior-round output) — the entire peer entry MUST be rendered as:

  ```
  **Peer-X**
  Verdict: <PASS|FAIL|NEEDS_WORK>
  Overall confidence: <0.0..1.0>

  Findings:
  - **<title>**
    <body>
  - **<title>**
    <body>
  ```

  where `Peer-X` is the opaque per-run ID, `<PASS|FAIL|NEEDS_WORK>` is the peer's verdict literal, `<0.0..1.0>` is the peer's `overall_confidence` rendered as a decimal in `[0,1]`, and each `- **<title>**\n  <body>` pair renders one finding (title on its own line as a bold list item, body indented two spaces on the following line(s); a multi-line body keeps the two-space indent on every continuation line). When the peer's `findings` array is empty, the entire `Findings:` block MUST be rendered as the single literal line `Findings: (none)` (no list items, no trailing blank line introduced by the empty list). Per-finding `confidence` and per-peer `summary` MUST NOT appear in this rendered shape (they remain in the underlying JSON for telemetry but are excluded from the rendered peer block per the deny-list / exclusion contract above). **Abstained-peer skeleton** (used when the peer abstained in the prior round) — the entire peer entry MUST be rendered as the two-line block:

  ```
  **Peer-X**
  (peer abstained)
  ```

  with no `findings` list, no `Verdict:` line, and no `Overall confidence:` line; the literal string `(peer abstained)` is the entire body of that peer's entry. The rendered string contains no model name, which is what makes R1's deny-list trivially apply over the placeholder.
- **Verification:**
  - **Given** two reviewers A and B produced labelled outputs in Round 1 each containing their model name in the body, **when** Round 2 prompt is constructed for reviewer A, **then** none of the deny-listed strings appear anywhere in the peer block, and the peer is identified only by an opaque ID.
  - **Given** a debate run with 3 reviewers, **when** Round 2 prompts are rendered, **then** each prompt contains exactly two peer blocks identified only by opaque IDs and the rendered text contains zero substrings from the deny-list, and **when** prompts are constructed for different recipients, **then** the order of peer entries differs per recipient (or, with only one other peer, ordering is trivially preserved and the test asserts exclusion not order).
  - **Given** a Round 2 peer block, **when** it is inspected, **then** per-finding `confidence` and per-peer `summary` are absent from the displayed peer block (they remain in JSON outputs for telemetry).
  - **Given** the deny-list is updated via a constant in the script, **when** a fixture-based test re-runs, **then** anonymization passes for the new entries without further code change.
  - **Given** the pinned POSIX ERE deny-list under `grep -oiE` with the canonical `(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])<term>($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])` word-boundary form, **when** a peer body containing each of the following tokens is scrubbed, **then** every listed token MUST be replaced (case-insensitive whole-word match):
    - `GPT-4` → match (the `-` is a non-word character and matches the trailing `[^A-Za-z0-9_]` group; the `GPT` segment is redacted)
    - `gpt-4o` → match (case-insensitive matches the `gpt` segment; `-` matches the trailing non-word group)
    - `Claude-3.5` → match (`-` matches the trailing non-word group; the `Claude` segment is redacted)
    - `Anthropic/Claude` → match (both `Anthropic` and `Claude` are matched independently; `/` is a non-word character on both sides)
    - `Gemini 1.5 Pro` → match (`Gemini` matches with the leading-of-line or whitespace alternative on the left and whitespace on the right)
    - `OpenAI's response` → match (`OpenAI` matches; the apostrophe is a non-word character because `'` is not in `[A-Za-z0-9_]`)
  - **Given** the pinned canonical loop form (boundary-preserving `\1[REDACTED]\<n>` substitution run iteratively until idempotent) and a peer body containing two adjacent deny-list terms separated by exactly one boundary character, **when** anonymization runs over input `Claude Codex`, **then** the output MUST have both `Claude` and `Codex` redacted (each replaced by `[REDACTED]` with the inter-term space preserved). A single non-iterative pass is non-conformant — a non-iterative implementation that leaves `Codex` unredacted (because the first match consumed the shared space as its trailing boundary) MUST fail this verification.
  - **Given** the same pinned canonical loop form, **when** anonymization runs over input `Claude Codex Gemini`, **then** all three tokens MUST be redacted in the output regardless of the platform (BSD or GNU `sed`) the loop runs under — i.e., the loop MUST converge (input == output across consecutive passes) only after every adjacent deny-list term has been replaced.
  - **Given** the same pinned regex, **when** a peer body containing each of the following counter-example tokens is scrubbed, **then** none of these MUST be replaced (whole-word boundary fails inside a longer alphanumeric word):
    - `claudication` → NO match (contains the substring `claud` but not the whole word `claude`; `c` after `claud` is a word character, so the trailing `($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])` group fails)
    - `gptcache` → NO match (no non-word character between `gpt` and `cache`)
    - `geminids` → NO match (the meteor shower; `geminid` extends `gemini` with a word character)
    - `agent13` → NO match (no boundary between `agent` and `13`; the deny-list term `Agent 1` requires the literal space)
  - **Given** a Round-1 active (non-abstained) peer with opaque ID `Peer-X`, verdict `V ∈ {PASS, FAIL, NEEDS_WORK}`, `overall_confidence = C`, and a non-empty `findings` array `[{title: T1, body: B1}, {title: T2, body: B2}, ...]` (per-finding `confidence` and per-peer `summary` ignored for rendering), **when** the Round-2 (or Round-3 under `--mode max`) prompt is constructed for a non-abstained reviewer, **then** that peer's entry within the anonymized peer block is rendered byte-for-byte as the active-peer skeleton pinned in the Requirement: `**Peer-X**\nVerdict: <V>\nOverall confidence: <C>\n\nFindings:\n- **<T1>**\n  <B1>\n- **<T2>**\n  <B2>\n...` (literal LF line endings, no trailing whitespace, two-space body indent on each finding-body line including continuations) — modulo only the deny-list redactions applied to titles and bodies per R1's scrub contract and the variable per-peer values (`Peer-X`, `<V>`, `<C>`, finding titles and bodies). Cross-reference: this is the active-peer counterpart to the abstained-peer skeleton verified below; every peer entry in the rendered block is exactly one of these two shapes.
  - **Given** a Round-1 active peer with opaque ID `Peer-X` and an empty `findings` array (`findings = []`), **when** the next-round prompt is constructed, **then** that peer's entry is rendered as the active-peer skeleton with the `Findings:` block collapsed to the single literal line `Findings: (none)` — i.e., `**Peer-X**\nVerdict: <V>\nOverall confidence: <C>\n\nFindings: (none)` — with no list items and no trailing blank line introduced by the empty list.
  - **Given** a Round-1 abstainer with opaque ID `Peer-X`, **when** the Round-2 prompt is constructed for a non-abstained reviewer, **then** that reviewer's anonymized peer block contains exactly the two-line entry `**Peer-X**\n(peer abstained)` (literal LF, no trailing whitespace) for the abstained slot, with no `findings` list, no `verdict`, and no `overall_confidence` rendered for `Peer-X`, and the literal string `(peer abstained)` is the entire body of that peer's entry.
  - **Given** the same scenario, **when** the receiving reviewer's Round-2 JSON output is parsed, **then** `peer_responses_seen` includes `Peer-X`'s opaque ID alongside the active peers' IDs (per R9's pinned semantics — the array reflects every peer slot the coordinator presented, not what the reviewer chose to engage with).
- **Edge cases:** A peer body that legitimately quotes itself by model name → MUST still be stripped at the peer-block boundary; the original finding body in the JSON output is unscrubbed (the deny-list is a presentation-time filter, not a content filter). Peer body whose embedded JSON contains a reviewer ID field → MUST still be stripped. A peer that produced zero output / abstained in any prior round (per the terminal-abstention rule in Section 2's Reviewer-abstain key state and "Reviewer crash mid-round" alternate flow — abstention is terminal for the run, so a Round-1 abstainer remains abstained in Round 2 and, under `--mode max`, in Round 3 as well) → MUST surface as `(peer abstained)` to active peers in every subsequent round, rather than be silently skipped, so absence vs. silence is unambiguous to the receiving reviewer. The peer-block construction set for round k+1 is therefore "every reviewer that participated in any prior round 1..k", with each entry rendered as either the active-peer skeleton (if the peer produced parseable, non-abstained output in round k specifically) or the abstained-peer skeleton (if the peer abstained in round k or in any earlier round j < k under the terminal-abstention rule). **Abstained-peer placeholder rendering shape (pinned).** The peer block presented to a reviewer is rendered text (markdown), not raw JSON — the active-peer entries render `findings` (title + body), `verdict`, and `overall_confidence` as rendered fields. The canonical shape for an abstained peer is therefore a rendered-text shape, not a JSON shape: for an abstained peer with opaque ID `Peer-X`, the entire peer-block entry MUST be rendered as the peer header on its own line followed by the literal string `(peer abstained)` on the next line as the entire body of that peer entry — i.e., the two-line block `**Peer-X**\n(peer abstained)` (where `\n` is a literal LF). No `findings` list, no `verdict`, and no `overall_confidence` are rendered for an abstained peer; the literal string `(peer abstained)` is the entire body of that peer's entry. This rendered shape (the literal string contains no model name) is what makes R1's deny-list trivially apply over the placeholder. **Telemetry / JSON shape (cross-ref R9).** No separate JSON shape is introduced for an abstained peer in the receiving reviewer's view — the rendering layer is what the reviewer sees, not the JSON. The abstained peer's opaque ID still appears in the receiving reviewer's `peer_responses_seen` array (per R9's pinned semantics: the array reflects the coordinator's input contract — every peer slot the coordinator presented, including abstained slots — not the reviewer's self-reported claim). Empty peer findings (`findings=[]`) → the peer block still appears with verdict + overall_confidence and an empty findings list. Opaque peer IDs are stable for the duration of one debate run; they reset between runs (no cross-run leakage). Deterministic ordering needed for replay tests vs. randomized for production runs (controlled by a hidden `--debate-seed N` CLI flag used by fixture tests only — undocumented in user-facing help; no new env vars are introduced, keeping the Non-goals env-var rule intact). **Seeded peer-ordering algorithm (pinned for cross-platform byte-stable fixtures).** The peer ordering presented to each recipient under `--debate-seed N` MUST be computed by the following deterministic algorithm so that fixture tests cannot overfit to one implementation and so that macOS (BSD `shasum`) and Linux (GNU `sha256sum`) produce byte-identical orderings: (1) For the recipient `R` (a canonical reviewer name from `{claude, codex, gemini}`) and each peer `P` in the peer set for that recipient (peers are the other reviewers that participated in the prior round; per the terminal-abstention rule above, this set includes both active prior-round peers and prior-round abstainers — abstainers still surface as `(peer abstained)` per the abstained-peer skeleton, but their position in the order is computed from the same algorithm), compute the key `K(R, P) = sha256("<seed>:<R-canonical-name>:<P-opaque-ID>")` where `<seed>` is the integer value of `N` rendered as its decimal string, `<R-canonical-name>` is the recipient's canonical alphabetical name (`claude`, `codex`, or `gemini`), `<P-opaque-ID>` is the peer's opaque per-run ID assigned in this requirement (e.g., `Peer-A`, `Peer-B`), and the colons are literal ASCII `0x3A` bytes. The hash MUST be computed via the same SHA-256 helper used by R3 (i.e., `shasum -a 256` preferred, falling back to `sha256sum` if `shasum` is absent on `PATH`, hard-error if neither is present — silent fallback to a non-cryptographic hash is forbidden). (2) Sort the peer set ascending by `K(R, P)` rendered as the lowercase 64-character hex digest, using lexicographic byte-order over the hex string (which, because hex digits are a strict subset of `[0-9a-f]` with consistent byte width, is equivalent to ascending numeric order over the 256-bit digest). (3) **Tiebreak:** at equal `K(R, P)` values (effectively unreachable with SHA-256, but documented for safety), break ties by peer-opaque-ID lexicographic ascending order. The hex-string comparison MUST be byte-comparison, not locale-aware string collation, so platforms with different default locales produce identical orderings. **Production (no `--debate-seed`) ordering source.** When `--debate-seed N` is absent (the production case), the implementation MUST use a non-deterministic source for the per-recipient shuffle — concretely, the Bash `$RANDOM` builtin or a `/dev/urandom`-derived value (e.g., `od -An -N4 -tu4 < /dev/urandom`) per recipient — so that production traffic genuinely shuffles per recipient and the peer ordering varies across otherwise-identical runs of the same artifact. The non-deterministic source MUST NOT be derived from any byte-parity-protected input (`<artifact_id>`, reviewer canonical names, peer opaque IDs, etc.) so that two production runs over the same artifact produce different per-recipient orderings, which is the property the randomization is meant to provide. **Byte-stable fixture contract.** When `--debate-seed N` IS present, the algorithm above is the single canonical form; both macOS and Linux MUST produce the same ordering for the same `(N, recipient, peer-set)` tuple, and fixture tests MAY assert byte-equality of the rendered peer block across both platforms. A test that captures a peer block under `--debate-seed N` on macOS and replays it under the same seed on Linux MUST observe a byte-identical peer block (modulo only the deny-list redactions, which are themselves byte-stable per R1's pinned regex flavor). Cross-reference: R9's `--debate-seed N` entry pins this same algorithm by reference; this is the canonical definition. **Regex-flavor portability:** the canonical pinned form is the explicit non-word-character class `(^|[^A-Za-z0-9_])<term>($|[^A-Za-z0-9_])` because it is the only word-boundary spelling accepted by both BSD grep (macOS) and GNU grep (Linux) under POSIX ERE — BSD-only `[[:<:]]` / `[[:>:]]` and GNU-only `\<` / `\>` / `\b` are explicitly NOT used and a literal implementation of either would fail on the other platform before any anonymization runs. If a future implementation switches to a regex engine that does not recognize POSIX ERE character classes (e.g., a pure-jq path), it MUST express the same semantics (a non-word-character or string boundary on each side of the deny-list term) and the fixture above MUST continue to pass byte-for-byte. The R1 deny-list test MUST be exercised on both macOS (BSD grep) and Linux (GNU grep) fixtures — either by running the `bin/tests/` deny-list test under both platforms in CI or by checking in pre-captured platform-specific scrub outputs and asserting they are byte-identical.

**R2 — Calibrated confidence elicitation and consumption** *(Core)*
- **Requirement:** Each reviewer prompt under `--debate` MUST include the **canonical calibration anchor block reproduced verbatim below** and MUST require both per-finding `confidence ∈ [0,1]` and an `overall_confidence ∈ [0,1]`. The block bytes — the literal characters between the fence markers, including line endings (LF only, no trailing whitespace) and any blank lines that appear between the fence markers — MUST appear identically in every reviewer prompt rendered under `--debate` across all four review types (`code`, `plan`, `spec`, `epic-verify`). The byte-equality contract is over exactly the content between the fence markers shown below: no leading blank line precedes the first non-blank line of the block, and no trailing blank line follows the last non-blank line of the block. Surrounding template glue (markdown headers, section ordering, other prompt instructions) MAY differ across templates; the bytes of the anchor block itself MUST NOT. The block is the single shared asset and MUST be sourced from one checked-in location (`prompts/strategies/confidence-anchors.md`, materialized as a deliverable in the launch checklist) so the four review-type templates cannot drift. - **Requirement:** Each reviewer assignment under `--debate` MUST include a **reasoning-strategy directive** drawn from the v1 set `[verification-first, falsification-first, decompose]` (fixed array, indexed 0/1/2 in that order). The implementation MUST prefer `shasum -a 256` (the macOS default; also available on most Linux distros that ship Perl), and MUST fall back to `sha256sum` (the Linux coreutils default; absent on stock macOS) when `shasum` is not present on `PATH`. If neither tool is available on `PATH`, the implementation MUST hard-error with a clear message naming both tools and the platform-default expectation, and MUST NOT silently fall back to a non-cryptographic hash, a Bash-builtin checksum, or any platform-specific alternative — silent fallback would break the per-(artifact, reviewer) determinism contract on any system where neither tool is present. With N ≤ 3 active reviewers a free slot is always reachable; with N=4+ the walk wraps and reviewers begin to share strategies (see edge cases). This makes assignment per-(artifact, reviewer) deterministic: swapping one reviewer can only change that reviewer's strategy slot directly, plus any deterministic shift forced by collision resolution against the unchanged reviewers (see edge cases). The directive MUST be sourced from a single shared asset so phrasing is byte-identical across all four review types.

- **Dedup predicate (deterministic, Bash 3.2 + jq feasible — no LLM call, no fuzzy/semantic similarity):** Two findings A and B from different reviewers MUST be considered duplicates **if and only if all** of the following hold:
  1. `A.priority == B.priority` (same priority — P0 with P0, P1 with P1, etc.; never cross-priority).
  2. Both have non-null `file_path` AND `A.file_path == B.file_path` (exact match, case-sensitive).
  3. Both have non-null `line_start` AND non-null `line_end` AND the integer ranges `[A.line_start, A.line_end]` and `[B.line_start, B.line_end]` overlap (i.e., `A.line_start <= B.line_end AND B.line_start <= A.line_end`).
  4. `lowercase(A.title) == lowercase(B.title)` after stripping the leading `[Px]` priority tag (if present) and surrounding whitespace from each title before comparison. **Pinned `[Px]` strip pattern (POSIX ERE):** `^[[:space:]]*\[P[0-3]\][[:space:]]*` — i.e., zero or more leading whitespace characters, a literal `[P` followed by exactly one digit in the set `{0,1,2,3}`, a literal `]`, then zero or more trailing whitespace characters. **Valid priority tags:** `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3` are the only valid priority levels (matching the four-level ladder used elsewhere in this spec); no other digit, letter, multi-digit, or word inside the brackets matches the strip pattern. **Strip is applied at most once per title:** the regex is anchored to `^` and is applied as a single non-recursive substitution, so a title like `"[P1] [P2] foo"` strips only the leading `[P1]` (yielding `"[P2] foo"` for comparison); a title with no leading `[Px]` tag is compared verbatim (after only whitespace trim and case-fold). After the strip, surrounding whitespace is trimmed (`^[[:space:]]+` and `[[:space:]]+$`) and the result is lowercased before equality comparison.
- If ANY of the above conditions fails, A and B are NOT duplicates and remain as distinct findings in `aggregate.json`. Findings with null `file_path`, null `line_start`, or null `line_end` are never deduplicated against any other finding — they remain distinct (this avoids the brittle case where one reviewer omits location and another doesn't).
- **Canonical merge order (deterministic, jq-iteration-order-independent).** Line-range overlap is non-transitive: A may overlap B and B may overlap C while A does not overlap C. Without a fixed merge order the final aggregated `findings[]` would arbitrarily depend on jq's internal iteration order over the candidate findings — non-deterministic across jq versions or input shuffles. The aggregator MUST therefore apply the dedup predicate in the following pinned order so the output is byte-deterministic regardless of jq iteration order: (1) **Global pre-sort** — collect all candidate findings from the active final-round reviewers' outputs into a single flat list and sort that list globally by the composite key `(priority asc, confidence desc, reviewer canonical name asc)`, where `priority asc` orders P0 before P1 before P2 before P3 (so the highest-priority finding sorts first), `confidence desc` places higher confidence earlier within the same priority, and `reviewer canonical name asc` (`claude` < `codex` < `gemini`) breaks ties at equal priority and equal confidence. The sort MUST be stable so equal keys preserve some deterministic relative order; jq's `sort_by` over a stable composite key satisfies this. (2) **Greedy fold** — iterate the globally-sorted list in order. Maintain a list of "primaries" (initially empty). For each finding F in iteration order: if F has not already been merged into an earlier primary, mark F itself as a new primary and append F to the primaries list; then scan all subsequent (lower-priority / lower-confidence / later-tied-reviewer) findings in the sorted list that have not yet been merged, and for each such F'' that satisfies the four-condition dedup predicate against F, merge F'' into F (i.e., F absorbs F''; F'' is removed from further consideration as a primary). After the iteration completes, the primaries list — in its iteration order — is the canonical deduplicated `findings[]` array written to `aggregate.json`. (3) **Properties guaranteed by this fold.** This single-pass greedy fold is jq-iteration-order-independent because every step operates on the pre-sorted list whose order is determined entirely by the composite key, not by jq's internal iteration. The highest-confidence body within each merge group is selected as primary by construction — the global pre-sort places it before all other candidates in that group, so it becomes the primary on first encounter. Non-transitive cases resolve in a single pass: if A overlaps B and B overlaps C but A does not overlap C, then on iterating A we merge B into A (A and B satisfy the predicate); when we reach C in the iteration, B has already been absorbed into A and is no longer a candidate primary, but C still satisfies the predicate against A only if A overlaps C, which by hypothesis it does not — so C becomes its own primary. The output has A as one primary (with B merged in) and C as a separate primary, deterministically, regardless of which order jq would have visited A, B, C absent the pre-sort.
- **Merge rule when F'' is merged into primary F (consistent with the canonical fold above):** F's body is kept as the primary body (the global pre-sort by `confidence desc` guarantees F has the higher confidence at fold time, and the alphabetical reviewer-name tiebreak applies at equal confidence so `claude` < `codex` < `gemini`); F''`s body is appended under an "Also raised by another reviewer" subsection of F's `body` field. The merged finding inherits F's `priority`, `title`, `file_path`, `line_start`, `line_end`, and `confidence`. The set union of reviewer names that raised the duplicate set is recorded as a new optional field `raised_by` on the merged finding (additive only, present in `aggregate.json` only, absent in non-debate runs and absent from per-reviewer JSONs). When ≥3 findings are merged into a single primary (e.g., F absorbs both F'' and F'''), each absorbed body is appended in fold-iteration order under the "Also raised by another reviewer" subsection, and `raised_by` is the full set union of reviewer names across all merged candidates.
- **Implementation note:** the predicate AND the canonical merge order are implementable in pure shell + jq — `sort_by` over the composite key for the pre-sort, then a single jq `reduce` over the sorted list for the greedy fold (each step folds matching subsequent candidates into the current primary). No LLM call, no fuzzy similarity, no embedding model. The aggregator runs as shell + jq, period.

- **Edge cases:** Title comparison is case-folded (`lowercase(A.title) == lowercase(B.title)`) and is performed **after** stripping the leading `[Px]` priority tag matched by the pinned POSIX ERE pattern `^[[:space:]]*\[P[0-3]\][[:space:]]*` (i.e., the priority levels are exactly `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3` — no other digits, no `Pending`, no `[P12]`), then trimming surrounding whitespace from each title; the strip is applied at most once per title (the regex is anchored to `^` and a single non-recursive substitution, so `"[P1] [P2] foo"` strips only the leading `[P1]`) and is purely a comparison-time normalization that does not modify the stored title in either the per-reviewer JSON or the merged `aggregate.json` finding. The `raised_by` field on a merged finding is the set union (deduplicated) of reviewer canonical names that produced the duplicates; it is additive-only, present in `aggregate.json` only, and absent in non-debate runs and absent from per-reviewer JSONs. The earlier output remains in telemetry only. The `raised_by` set therefore only ever contains canonical names of active final-round reviewers. Confidence is not used to override the priority field of a finding. Tied confidences in an `aggregate.json` verdict tiebreak fall back to today's deterministic tiebreak (e.g., reviewer order); the Stop-hook's per-reviewer consensus calculation uses its existing tiebreak logic unchanged. Tied confidences in a near-duplicate merge resolve deterministically by alphabetical reviewer canonical-name order (`claude` < `codex` < `gemini`). Missing confidence MUST NOT be treated as stronger than explicit confidence. Priority blocking applies post-dedup in `aggregate.json` and per-reviewer in the Stop-hook: cross-priority merge is forbidden by the predicate (so a P1 is never folded into a P0 in the aggregate), and the per-reviewer P1 still triggers `requires_decision` via the Stop-hook regardless of how it was rendered in the aggregate.

  - **Given** the canonical calibration anchor block — the fenced literal reproduced in R2 and checked into `prompts/strategies/confidence-anchors.md` — **when** it is rendered into each of the four reviewer templates (`prompts/reviewers/code.md`, `prompts/reviewers/plan.md`, `prompts/reviewers/spec.md`, `prompts/reviewers/epic-verify.md`) under `--debate`, **then** the substring of each rendered prompt corresponding to the anchor block is byte-identical to `prompts/strategies/confidence-anchors.md` and byte-identical across all four templates (the comparison is `cmp`-equal on the extracted block region, not a phrasing or whitespace-tolerant match). Surrounding template glue (markdown headers, section ordering) is permitted to differ across templates; the anchor block bytes are not.
  - **Given** a strategy directive (e.g., `decompose`), **when** it is rendered into each of the four reviewer templates under `--debate`, **then** the directive bytes are identical across all four templates.
- **Edge cases:** `spawn-epic-verify` reviewers also explore the codebase via tool use — debate's larger Round-2 prompt size MUST not blow per-reviewer tool-call budgets; this is a manual smoke test on a representative epic. Slash-command wrappers (under `commands/cerberus/review-*.md`) routing into `spawn-*-review` MUST pass `--debate` through to `bin/review-gate` if specified — the named-subcommand path always carries the right `--type`, so this is plumbing only and the wrapper does not need to validate. Revision prompts in `prompts/revisions/` MUST not accidentally inherit debate transcript state. Generator prompts in `prompts/generators/` MUST not require confidence or strategy directives. A user who passes `--debate` alongside `--max-rounds` (a pre-existing outer-loop flag) gets independent semantics: `--max-rounds` controls outer iterations, `--debate` controls inner rounds within one iteration. The architecture-review and healthcheck templates remain out of v1 — they go through `bin/generate`, not `bin/review-gate spawn`, and `--debate` on those generator surfaces is silently ignored or fails downstream per the rejection-layer commitment above; v2 may add wrapper-level rejection or extend debate to other surfaces. A user who passes `--debate` to bare `spawn` and relies on auto-detection of `--type` (`auto`) MUST be rejected at preflight — `auto` is explicitly outside the four-judging-type whitelist; users wanting debate must pass an explicit judging `--type` or use a named `spawn-*-review` subcommand.

## 4. Instrumentation & Release Checks

**Instrumentation** *(v1, lightweight)*
- Per-round, per-reviewer: strategy assigned, `overall_confidence` reported, abstain flag, integrity flags (e.g., out-of-range confidence clamped, missing confidence field).
- Per-run: total inner rounds executed (always 2 in v1 unless `--mode max`, where it's 3), wall-clock per round, total token usage per reviewer per round (extracted from existing reviewer telemetry plumbing if available; else best-effort).
- Aggregator notes (e.g., "tiebreak by confidence", "near-duplicate merged", "reviewer X clamped out-of-range confidence").
- `gate-state.json` is extended with a `debate` block, present only when `--debate` is set, capturing the above. **Canonical top-level shape (literal skeleton):** the block MUST follow the shape below; field names are pinned, and missing optional fields (e.g., `tokens_used` when not extractable from a reviewer's telemetry) MUST use JSON `null` rather than being omitted so consumers can write a stable parser.

  ```json
  {
    "debate": {
      "rounds": 2,
      "mode": "smart",
      "consensus_mode": "majority",
      "strategies": {
        "claude": "verification-first",
        "codex": "falsification-first",
        "gemini": "decompose"
      },
      "rounds_telemetry": [
        {
          "round": 1,
          "started_at": "2026-04-25T10:00:00Z",
          "finished_at": "2026-04-25T10:01:30Z",
          "wall_clock_ms": 90000,
          "reviewers": [
            {"name": "claude", "abstained": false, "overall_confidence": 0.8, "tokens_used": 1234, "integrity_flags": []},
            {"name": "codex", "abstained": false, "overall_confidence": 0.7, "tokens_used": 1500, "integrity_flags": []},
            {"name": "gemini", "abstained": true, "overall_confidence": null, "tokens_used": null, "integrity_flags": ["timeout"]}
          ]
        },
        {"round": 2, "started_at": "2026-04-25T10:01:30Z", "finished_at": "2026-04-25T10:03:00Z", "wall_clock_ms": 90000, "reviewers": [
          {"name": "claude", "abstained": false, "overall_confidence": 0.85, "tokens_used": 1300, "integrity_flags": []},
          {"name": "codex", "abstained": false, "overall_confidence": 0.7, "tokens_used": 1600, "integrity_flags": []}
        ]}
      ],
      "aggregator_notes": ["near-duplicate merged: P1 missing null check (claude+codex)"]
    }
  }
  ```

  Field semantics: `rounds` is the total inner rounds executed in the run (2 under `--mode fast`/`smart`, 3 under `--mode max`). `mode` is the resolved intelligence mode (`fast` | `smart` | `max`). `consensus_mode` is the resolved consensus rule (`majority` | `all` | `any`) — same value as in `aggregate.json` for the same run. `strategies` is the per-reviewer R3 strategy assignment for the run, keyed by canonical reviewer name; the keys cover every reviewer that was active in any round (including reviewers that abstained in the final peer round, since strategy assignment is computed once at run start). `rounds_telemetry[]` MUST contain exactly one entry per round actually executed and reaching aggregation (so under `--mode fast`/`smart` the array length is 2 when both rounds completed and aggregation succeeded; under `--mode max` it is 3). Each entry's `reviewers[]` lists every reviewer that was actually invoked for that round; an `abstained: true` entry indicates the reviewer abstained in that round per the Mode A rules in Section 2 — `overall_confidence` and `tokens_used` are `null` for abstained slots, and `integrity_flags` records the Mode A trigger (`"timeout"`, `"crash"`, `"unparseable_json"`) and any Mode B flags from confidence-field handling. **Terminal-abstention surfacing in `rounds_telemetry`:** because abstention is terminal for the run (Section 2's pinned eligibility rule — a reviewer that abstains in round k is NOT invoked in any subsequent round k+1, k+2, ...), a reviewer that abstained in round k appears as an `abstained: true` entry in round k's `reviewers[]` and is then **absent** from the `reviewers[]` arrays of all subsequent rounds (the reviewer was not invoked, so there is no `abstained: true` re-entry in later rounds). Implementations MAY optionally include a `"skipped: abstained earlier"` integrity-flag entry in subsequent rounds for clarity, but the canonical shape is simple absence: `rounds_telemetry[k].reviewers[]` lists exactly the reviewers invoked in round k+1 (which is the set of reviewers non-abstained across all prior rounds 1..k). Consumers MUST NOT infer that a reviewer absent from a later round's `reviewers[]` is "active" — absence indicates non-invocation, which under the terminal-abstention rule means the reviewer abstained earlier. `aggregator_notes[]` is an array of human-readable strings (e.g., near-duplicate merge notes, tiebreak notes); the wording of individual notes is not a stable schema and consumers MUST NOT pattern-match against it. **Write-path (success-only):** the `gate-state.json.debate` block is written ONLY on the success path — i.e., after the in-process aggregator completes and the per-reviewer review JSONs and `aggregate.json` are written into the reviews directory (Section 2 step 7). On SIGINT cancellation, on aggregator failure (Section 2 "Aggregator failure"), or on the degraded-below-2 hard error, `gate-state.json.debate` is NOT written: the coordinator exits without populating the `debate` block, and `gate-state.json` for the cancelled / failed / degraded run carries no `debate` key at all (consistent with Section 2's "no new debate-specific field is added to `gate-state.json` to mark in-progress debate state"). Partial round telemetry for those non-success paths lives instead in the per-iteration telemetry directory (`iterations/<iter>/`) as a separate file (e.g., `iterations/<iter>/debate-telemetry.json`); that separate file MAY have a `rounds_telemetry` array of length 1 (cancelled mid-Round-1, only Round 1 telemetry collected before the cancel/failure), length 2 (cancelled mid-Round-2 under `--mode max`, or aggregator failed after both peer rounds finished), etc., reflecting whatever the coordinator collected before the non-success exit. The per-iteration telemetry file is not consumed by the Stop-hook, the aggregator, or any subsequent decision surface; it is for inspection only. **Byte-parity (per R9):** the `debate` block is absent in non-debate runs — `gate-state.json` for a run without `--debate` MUST NOT contain a `debate` key at all (not `"debate": null`, not `"debate": {}`). The block appears only in `--debate` runs that complete the success path.
- Stored alongside existing review JSONs in the session-scoped directory at `~/.claude/projects/<project_hash>/cerberus/<session_id>/`.
- Gate report (Markdown summary returned by Stop-hook) gains a one-line "Debate: round N/N (strategies: verify/falsify/decompose)" indicator when debate ran (where N is 2 under `fast`/`smart` and 3 under `max`), a per-reviewer table that shows `overall_confidence` only (one column — per-finding confidence stays in JSON for telemetry), with a `Strategy` column **appended after the existing reviewer column (rightmost position)** so existing column positions are preserved. The Strategy column header is literally `Strategy`; values are the short forms `verify` / `falsify` / `decompose` (rendering of `verification-first` / `falsification-first` / `decompose`). The cross-reviewer-merged finding set is sourced from `aggregate.json` (one card per defect). The aggregate verdict is shown alongside the Stop-hook's authoritative gate verdict.
- No new dashboards. No K\*. No disagreement-rate metric. No sycophancy diagnostic. No per-run "no-debate counterfactual" comparison.

**Decisions made**
- **v1 primitives:** Core only — R1 (anonymization), R2 (confidence with anchors), R3 (strategy diversity), R6 (confidence-weighted aggregation that respects priority), R9 (CLI compat), R10 (per-type scope). Deferred to v2: R4 sparsification, R5 adaptive early exit, R7 diagnostics including K\*, R8 adversarial robustness, RedDebate-style cross-session memory.
- **Debate scope:** All four `spawn-*-review` named subcommands and bare `spawn` (the latter only when `--type ∈ {code, plan, spec, epic-verify}` — the canonical internal `REVIEW_TYPE` values; the `spawn-*-review` named subcommands are CLI sugar that set `--type` to the corresponding short name). Generators excluded.
- **Bare-`spawn` `--debate` whitelist & rejection-layer commitment:** v1 enforces the four-judging-type whitelist at `bin/review-gate` preflight only. `--debate` on bare `spawn` is accepted iff `--type` is one of the four canonical short-name judging types — `code`, `plan`, `spec`, or `epic-verify` — which are the existing internal `REVIEW_TYPE` values used end-to-end (env var, `mode.type` in `gate-state.json`, artifact frontmatter marker, `prompts/reviewers/<type>.md` selector, `prompts/revisions/<type>.md` selector). Any other `--type` (including `healthcheck`, `architecture-review`, `create-tasks`, `manual`, `auto`) is rejected with a clear preflight error before any model invocation. Slash-command markdown wrappers under `commands/cerberus/*.md` do NOT validate `--debate` placement in v1; generator slash commands receiving `--debate` will silently ignore it or fail downstream when `bin/generate` rejects the unknown flag, depending on the wrapper's pass-through behavior. Wrapper-level rejection in slash-command markdown is a deliberate v2 polish item, not a self-deferring open question.
- **Byte-parity exception scope:** The "no observable change without `--debate`" guarantee covers user-observed output of *valid invocations* only. Help output (`--help` / usage), version output, and error messages emitted for newly-rejected flag combinations introduced by this feature (e.g., `--debate` on a non-judging `--type`) are explicitly part of the new feature surface and are permitted to differ from the pre-feature baseline.
- **Rollout shape:** Opt-in `--debate` flag only. No env vars in v1, including no `REVIEW_DEBATE_DISABLED` kill switch — emergency rollback is "drop the flag from invocations" (or revert the plugin version).
- **Success metric:** v1 ships with the byte-parity criterion (no harm when off) plus one falsifiable acceptance signal (curated bad-artifact fixture, asserted as **two conjunctive clauses** — both required, deliberately not a single ∀-quantified statement that could vacuously pass when no Round-1 P1 exists at the planted location): **Clause 1 (existence in Round 1)** — at least one reviewer MUST raise a P1 finding F at the planted defect's location in Round 1 (with non-null `F.file_path` exactly matching the planted location and non-null `F.line_start`/`F.line_end` overlapping the planted range per R6's overlap predicate); **Clause 2 (retention in final-round aggregate)** — that same defect MUST be retained as F' in `aggregate.json` of the final peer round, where "retained" means there exists F' in the final findings with `priority == "P1"`, `confidence >= 0.7`, exact `file_path` match, and non-null line-range overlap with F per R6's overlap predicate; title match is NOT required. The ambiguous-artifact and `(peer abstained)` fixtures are additional vibes-checks, not gating. No formal numeric metric in v1 beyond the falsifiable signal. Recorded explicitly so v2 can revisit with a real fixture set or a sycophancy diagnostic. The earlier "vibes-only" framing was rejected as gameable: byte-parity alone permits a no-op `--debate` implementation (Round 2 prompt that ignores the peer block) to pass the launch checklist; the two-clause form closes both the no-op loophole (Clause 2's file_path + line-overlap retention rule), the secondary "drop the planted-defect P1 but emit any unrelated P1" loophole that a looser definition of "retained" would leave open (also Clause 2), and the vacuous-pass loophole where no Round-1 P1 ever raises at the planted location (Clause 1).
- **Round budget:** Fixed 2 rounds, mode-overridable. Defaults: `fast=2`, `smart=2`, `max=3`. No adaptive early exit in v1.
- **Confidence:** Self-reported only with calibration anchors in the prompt. No per-agent prior rescaling.
- **Strategy set:** `verification-first` / `falsification-first` / `decompose`. Shared phrasing across all review types. Single shared phrasing.
- **Strategy-directive asset location:** Strategy-directive language lives at `prompts/strategies/` with one short Markdown file per strategy (`verification-first.md`, `falsification-first.md`, `decompose.md`), included into reviewer prompts when `--debate` is on. This co-locates the strategy assets with the canonical calibration anchor block already pinned at `prompts/strategies/confidence-anchors.md`, so `prompts/strategies/` is the single established home for shared `--debate` prompt assets — reviewer templates under `prompts/reviewers/*.md` reference these files rather than embedding the directive text inline, keeping phrasing byte-identical across all four review types per R3.
- **Strategy assignment:** Per-`(artifact_id, reviewer-canonical-name)` hash → stable per-(artifact, reviewer) assignment across runs. Different artifacts shuffle. **Per-review-type `<artifact_id>`** (the canonical hash input — pinned per type because the session-scoped `$review_dir/latest.md` path varies per session and so cannot be the hash input): plan-review → `realpath` of the user-passed plan file; spec-review → `realpath` of the user-passed spec file; code-review → the verbatim `diff_args_str` (e.g., `--uncommitted`, `--base main`, `--commit <sha>,<sha>`) since code-review has no single source file; epic-verify → `realpath` of the epic file when invoked with a file argument, otherwise the verbatim raw-criteria string passed on the command line (matching the `epic_context` value the spawn flow already computes). **Hash primitive:** SHA-256, computed via `shasum -a 256` when present (macOS default; available on most Linux distros that ship Perl) and falling back to `sha256sum` (Linux coreutils default) when `shasum` is absent. Selection is at runtime via `command -v` — `shasum` preferred, `sha256sum` fallback, hard-error if neither is available (no silent fallback to a non-cryptographic hash). Both tools emit the same 64-hex digest format; the implementation reads the first 64 hex characters of stdout regardless of which ran. Bash 3.2 portable on both platforms. **Index derivation:** first 8 hex chars of the digest, parsed as a base-16 unsigned integer, modulo `len(strategies)` (3 in v1), mapped through the fixed array `[verification-first, falsification-first, decompose]`. **Canonical reviewer order** for collision resolution is alphabetical (`claude`, `codex`, `gemini`); collisions resolve by forward walk `(index+1) mod 3, (index+2) mod 3`. Strict per-pair stability holds when no collision occurs; collision-driven shifts are deterministic and observable in telemetry. Drops the older "permutation over active set" framing — that scheme couldn't simultaneously deliver per-pair stability under reviewer-set changes.
- **R3 collision-driven shift acceptance:** Collision-driven shifts to a previously-unchanged reviewer's strategy slot (when swapping one reviewer triggers a canonical-order forward-walk that lands on the unchanged reviewer's slot) are explicitly accepted as v1 behavior. The shifts are deterministic, observable in telemetry (the assignment is recorded per round and surfaced in the gate report's Strategy column), and reproducible from the `(artifact_id, reviewer-set)` inputs alone. A stronger policy that anchors unchanged reviewers first (e.g., a stateful per-(artifact, reviewer-set) assignment cache) was considered and rejected for v1 because it would require state persistence beyond the current stateless `shasum`-only scheme and would not compose cleanly with the existing per-(artifact, reviewer) hash determinism. Revisit in v2 if telemetry surfaces meaningful user surprise; the rejected option is recorded here so v2 doesn't re-derive it.
- **Anonymization aggressiveness:** Strict deny-list of model + agent labels. No stylometric flattening, no JSON-shape normalization in v1.
- **Confidence fallback and abstain handling:** Two distinct reviewer-failure modes are handled with distinct rules, applied identically in Section 2 alternate flows and R2 edge cases. **Mode A (crash / timeout / unparseable JSON, OR parseable JSON missing `findings` or `verdict`):** reviewer abstains for the round entirely (`abstained=true`); findings/verdict/confidence are absent and contribute nothing to aggregation for that round (no 0.0 or 0.5 placeholder). **Terminal-abstention rule (pinned, applied uniformly across Section 2 key states, Section 2 Coordinator flow steps 4-5, Section 2 "Reviewer crash mid-round" alternate flow, R1 peer-block construction, R6 degraded-below-2 trigger, and `gate-state.json.debate.rounds_telemetry`):** a reviewer that abstains in round k is NOT invoked in any subsequent round k+1, k+2, ... — it remains abstained for the rest of the run. Rationale: a Round-1 crash/timeout is a strong signal the reviewer is unhealthy for this run; retrying it in Round 2 risks duplicating the failure mode and wasting time/tokens, and treating abstention as terminal avoids partial-state edge cases (e.g., a Round-1-abstained reviewer that succeeds in Round 2 — what's its `overall_confidence`? does the Round-2 prompt include a self-block referencing absent Round-1 output?). The rejected alternative (re-invoke an abstained reviewer in a later round) was considered and rejected on those grounds. The abstained reviewer's slot still surfaces to active peers in subsequent rounds as `(peer abstained)` per R1 (preserving the round-shape signal for active peers), and abstained reviewers' opaque IDs still appear in `peer_responses_seen` for active reviewers per R9. The terminal-abstention rule composes cleanly with the degraded-below-2 trigger: at the start of any round k, if fewer than 2 reviewers are non-abstained across all prior rounds 1..k-1, the coordinator hard-errors before launching round k (e.g., 2 reviewers + 1 Round-1 abstention → no Round 2 launch; 3 reviewers + 2 distinct abstentions across Rounds 1 and 2 under `--mode max` → no Round 3 launch). **Final-round abstain — Option B (exclude from final aggregation, chosen):** a reviewer that abstains in the final peer round (Round 2 under `fast`/`smart`, Round 3 under `max`) is fully excluded from the final aggregation. No per-reviewer review JSON is written for them into the reviews directory, they do NOT contribute to `aggregate.json`, and the Stop-hook does NOT see them. Their earlier-round output (e.g., a Round 1 output that exists when Round 2 abstained) is preserved in telemetry only and is NEVER carried forward into the canonical outputs. Option A (carry forward the last-valid round's output with explicit `round=K-1` metadata) was considered and rejected because it would mix rounds inside `aggregate.json` and contradict R6's "consumes the final peer round" framing; the rejected option is recorded here so v2 doesn't re-derive it. **Mode B (parseable JSON with `findings` and `verdict` present, but missing or out-of-range confidence fields ONLY):** missing `overall_confidence` → `0.5` (uncertain default — distinguishes "didn't answer" from "had nothing to say"); missing per-finding `confidence` → `0.5`; out-of-range values clamped to `[0,1]`; integrity warning logged; reviewer is NOT abstained, findings still count. **Mode A vs. Mode B boundary (pinned):** Mode B is explicitly limited to the confidence fields (`overall_confidence` and per-finding `confidence`). Missing `findings` (the array itself absent — note that `findings: []` is valid Mode B output meaning "the reviewer saw no defects") or missing `verdict` is Mode A, not Mode B — the reasoning is that confidence is a debate-only addition with a sensible default whereas `findings` and `verdict` are the existing core schema that must be present for the reviewer to be considered to have produced output at all. Substituting defaults for missing `findings` / `verdict` was considered and rejected because it would silently inflate the active-reviewer count and let a reviewer that produced no content still count toward the `<2 active final-round reviewers` degraded trigger. R9's strict schema (`additionalProperties: false` with `findings`/`verdict` as required properties) should already reject these cases at schema-validation time, so this boundary is primarily defense-in-depth pinning the runtime semantics if such a JSON ever reaches the aggregator. **Active-reviewer count and degraded-below-2 trigger:** "active reviewer count" at the final peer round is the count of reviewers with a parseable, non-abstained output in that round; the gate fires its degraded-below-2 hard-error and lands in `requires_decision` (with the reason "debate degraded below 2 active reviewers in the final peer round") whenever this count is less than 2 once the final peer round resolves OR once it becomes mathematically impossible to reach 2 (e.g., abstentions in an earlier round leave fewer than 2 reviewers eligible to attempt the final round); the coordinator errors out before writing the reviews directory, no `aggregate.json` is written, and no per-reviewer JSONs are written. Abstained peers (Mode A) surface to subsequent-round peers as `(peer abstained)` in the anonymized peer block (per R1 edge cases) so absence is informative rather than invisible — preserving the round's information shape against silent collapse of disagreement. **Pinned rendered shape (cross-ref R1 edge cases):** the abstained-peer entry is rendered as the two-line block `**Peer-X**\n(peer abstained)` — peer header on its own line, then the literal string `(peer abstained)` as the entire body, with no `findings` / `verdict` / `overall_confidence` rendered. Because the placeholder is rendered text (not a JSON shape), no separate JSON shape is introduced; the abstained peer's opaque ID still appears in the receiving reviewer's `peer_responses_seen` per R9. The `(peer abstained)` placeholder contains no model name, so R1's deny-list anonymization is trivially satisfied over it.
- **P0/P1 priority gate:** Unchanged. Confidence cannot suppress a P0/P1 finding.
- **Verdict authority:** Single source of truth for gate pass/fail is the Stop-hook's existing priority-then-consensus calculator running over per-reviewer final-round JSONs (unchanged from today). The aggregator additionally produces an `aggregate.json` cross-reviewer-merged view that drives the gate report's one-card-per-defect rendering and supplies a confidence-tiebroken aggregate verdict reported alongside as context. Confidence is only used inside `aggregate.json` (cross-reviewer near-duplicate dedup + PASS/NEEDS_WORK tiebreak); per-reviewer JSONs are not cross-reviewer-deduped.
- **R6 dedup predicate:** Two findings from different reviewers are duplicates **iff** all of: (1) `priority` equality, (2) non-null + exact-match `file_path` equality (case-sensitive), (3) non-null `line_start`/`line_end` on both sides AND integer line-range overlap (`A.line_start <= B.line_end AND B.line_start <= A.line_end`), (4) case-folded `title` equality after stripping the leading `[Px]` priority tag and trimming surrounding whitespace. Findings with null `file_path`/`line_start`/`line_end` are never deduped against any other finding. **Canonical merge order (pinned to make the output jq-iteration-order-independent in the face of non-transitive line-range overlap).** Pre-sort all candidate findings globally by the composite key `(priority asc, confidence desc, reviewer canonical name asc)`, then greedily fold: iterate the sorted list, mark each not-yet-merged F as a new primary, and merge any subsequent matching F'' into F. The pre-sort guarantees the highest-confidence body in each merge group becomes primary by construction, and the single-pass greedy fold resolves non-transitive overlaps deterministically (A may overlap B but not C; on the sorted-iteration pass A absorbs B, and C surfaces as its own primary because by then B is no longer a candidate primary and A does not overlap C). Alphabetical reviewer order (`claude` < `codex` < `gemini`) is the documented tiebreak when priority and confidence are equal; the non-primary body is appended under "Also raised by another reviewer". A new optional additive `raised_by` field on the merged finding records the reviewer-name set union (present in `aggregate.json` only). Implementable in pure shell + jq (`sort_by` for the pre-sort, `reduce` for the fold) — no LLM call, no fuzzy/semantic similarity.
- **Aggregator round target:** Aggregator consumes the **final peer round's** outputs — Round 2 under `--mode fast` / `smart`, Round 3 under `--mode max`. Earlier round outputs are preserved in telemetry only.
- **Inner-round coordinator:** Under `--debate`, `bin/review-gate spawn` (and the four `spawn-*-review` variants) becomes a synchronous in-process coordinator: runs Round 1 reviewers in parallel → anonymizes in-process → runs Round 2 → (under `max`, anonymizes and runs Round 3) → runs the aggregator → only then writes per-reviewer review JSONs and `aggregate.json` into the reviews directory. The Stop-hook then operates as today. **Cancel/resume contract.** SIGINT mid-debate lets the in-flight round complete, persists partial round telemetry into the per-iteration directory, and exits without populating the reviews directory. `gate-state.json.status` stays at `"pending"` (the value `spawn` wrote on entry — the coordinator does NOT flip it to `"awaiting_decision"` on cancel; that transition is owned by the Stop-hook's consensus path which never runs without a populated reviews directory). No new debate-specific field is added to `gate-state.json` to mark in-progress debate state; the cancellation is observable purely from `status="pending"` plus the absence of `reviews/*.done`/`*.failed` sentinels. `bin/review-gate wait --json` returns `status: "timeout"` (exit 3) after `--timeout` elapses against the cancelled gate, or `status: "no_reviewers"` (exit 4) if the reviewers map is empty. v1 has no mid-debate resume — recovery is "re-run `spawn` with `--debate`", which starts a fresh Round 1 (the existing active-gate guard rejects this for 30 min unless `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is set on the re-spawn). `bin/review-gate resolve` is **optional** for recovery: it cleanly clears the cancelled gate to `status: "resolved"` / `decision.action = "proceed"` so the next `spawn` does not collide with the active-gate guard, but is not required if `REVIEW_GATE_RERUN=1` is used. Partial telemetry from the cancelled run is preserved on disk for inspection but is never consumed by the next run, the Stop-hook, or the aggregator.
- **Outer-loop carry:** Fresh start. No carry-over of debate transcript across gate iterations.
- **Degraded fallback:** Hard error if fewer than 2 reviewers are available when `--debate` is set.
- **Mode interaction:** Debate inherits `--mode`. Mode picks underlying models; debate adds rounds. They compose.
- **Round 2 peer block:** Each peer's `findings` (title + body) + `verdict` + `overall_confidence`. Per-peer `summary` and per-finding `confidence` are NOT in the displayed block; they remain in JSON for telemetry.
- **JSON schema additions:** Additive only. New optional fields when `--debate` is on: `overall_confidence`, per-finding `confidence`, `strategy`, `round`, `peer_responses_seen`. Absent in non-debate runs. **Schema artifact selection (per R9):** because Codex is invoked with a strict `--output-schema` whose `additionalProperties: false` would otherwise reject the new fields, `bin/review-gate` selects the schema variant conditionally on `--debate` — non-debate runs emit the pre-feature schema byte-for-byte; debate runs emit a debate variant that admits the new optional fields while keeping `additionalProperties: false` for unknown fields. The same conditional selection applies to the schema embedded in `repair_review_output()`'s repair prompt.
- **Reviewer prompt templates:** Updated to require confidence + accept a strategy directive when debate is on; absence of debate keeps templates' behavior byte-for-byte as today.
- **Reusing existing infrastructure:** `bin/review-gate`, `gate-state.json`, the reviews directory, the Stop-hook, the iteration telemetry path, the `--mode` resolver, the consensus calculator, the agent allowlist (`codex,gemini,claude`), the JSON output schema, the Gemini read-only policy.
- **Tier:** L. Complexity score = 8 (data-model addition, multi-flow impact across 4 review types, multi-agent integration, opt-in feature flag, per-prompt-template change, per-aggregator change). Recorded per the tier-disagreement protocol.
- **Gate report column layout:** The per-reviewer table in the gate report shows `overall_confidence` only (one column) — per-finding confidence stays in JSON for telemetry, keeping the gate report compact. The `Strategy` column is appended after the existing reviewer column (rightmost position) so existing column positions are preserved, minimizing parser surprise. Column header is literally `Strategy`; values are the short forms `verify` / `falsify` / `decompose` (rendering `verification-first` / `falsification-first` / `decompose`). Rendering per-finding confidence in the gate report was considered and rejected for v1 because it would bloat the table without changing gate decisions; v2 may revisit if a maintainer wants per-finding confidence inline.
- **Unchanged Round 2 UX:** A reviewer that emits a parseable Round 2 output identical (verdict + findings) to Round 1 is NOT abstained — it's a deliberate "no change" stance, recorded as a normal Round 2 output with no special indicator. The gate report does NOT distinguish "unchanged" from "changed" Round 2 outputs — the reviewer's final-round JSON is what counts (the Mode A / Mode B abstain rules above are the only abstain semantics). A "no change" telemetry diagnostic (e.g., a Round-2-equals-Round-1 marker for analysis) is explicitly a v2 concern.
- **Integrity-warning visibility:** Log-only in v1. Integrity warnings (out-of-range confidence clamped, missing confidence field, deny-list scrub firing inside a finding body that legitimately discusses a deny-listed term, etc.) go to iteration telemetry and stderr only. The gate report shows nothing about integrity unless the run was actually blocked (e.g., the `<2 active reviewers` hard error). Surfacing integrity counters in the gate report was considered and rejected for v1 because the gate report is for go/no-go decisions, not diagnostics; the iteration telemetry path is the right surface for the maintainer-facing diagnostic.
- **Golden-output JSON in v1 tests:** v1 fixture tests check in golden JSON outputs for the additive debate fields (`overall_confidence`, `findings[*].confidence`, `strategy`, `round`, `peer_responses_seen`) plus the `aggregate.json` shape. This catches schema drift across reviewer providers (Claude / Codex / Gemini) and across feature changes. The golden JSONs sit alongside the existing R1 / R3 / R6 fixtures in `bin/tests/fixtures/` and are asserted byte-equal by the corresponding fixture tests. Captured as a Launch checklist deliverable.

**Risks & mitigations**
- **Risk: token cost approximately 2× non-debate (3× under `max`).** This is a manual maintainer check, not a budget enforced in v1. Mitigation: the `<2 reviewers` hard-error keeps the cost floor honest; the fixed (not adaptive) round budget keeps the ceiling honest. README documents the cost band per mode.
- **Risk: wall-clock latency approximately 2× non-debate under fast/smart (3× under `max`).** Round 2 cannot start until Round 1 finishes (the coordinator is synchronous by construction in Section 2); debate adds at least one full reviewer round to the critical path, and two under `max`. This matters for CI timeouts more than for token spend — a CI job sized for a single-pass review will time out under `--debate`. Mitigation: README and `--help` document the latency band alongside the token band; users sizing CI timeouts must account for this. No timeout reshape in v1.
- **Risk: undocumented external markdown-parser of the gate report.** Anyone outside the repo who is regex-parsing the gate report markdown table (rather than consuming `wait --json`) will see the new Strategy column and the new debate indicator line. The repo's own consumers (`bin/review-gate-hook.sh` and humans) are column-tolerant; external parsers are undocumented and unsupported. Mitigation: the parser-identification clarification under Validation after release pins the supported consumers and recommends external orchestration migrate to `wait --json` (whose shape is unchanged in v1). Documented as a non-supported breakage path.
- **Risk: stylistic leakage despite anonymization.** Strict deny-list cannot defeat voice/prose-style fingerprinting. The maintainer deliberately accepted this on the explicit reasoning that Round 2 still captures the cross-model-evidence win even if reviewers occasionally guess "that sounded like Codex." Stylometric flattening was rejected as not paying for its complexity in v1.
- **Risk: Gemini read-only policy under peer broadcasts (laundering surface).** A peer block could in principle relay text that coaxes Gemini toward write tools. Mitigation: the read-only policy at `config/gemini-readonly-policy.toml` is enforced on every Gemini call regardless of debate state, the prompt template enforcing it is in the always-on layer, and a launch-checklist manual smoke verifies no jailbreak surface is opened by debate prompts. Flagged as an integrity-warning Open Question.
- **Risk: strategy directive accidentally degrading a reviewer.** A directive ("falsify the artifact") could in principle push a reviewer into adversarial-without-evidence mode. Mitigation: the directive language is anchored ("try to falsify" is paired with "and present concrete counterexamples"), it sits inside the always-on prompt template that enforces priority semantics and Gemini read-only, and the gate report exposes the assignment so a degraded reviewer is visible to the maintainer in the vibes-check.
- **Risk: confidence inflation across the board** (every reviewer emits 0.9). Accepted in v1; the anchor language is advisory not enforced, and per-agent rescaling is deliberately deferred to v2.
- **Risk: low cross-reviewer dedup hit rate from strict exact-title matching.** R6's dedup predicate requires `lowercase(A.title) == lowercase(B.title)` (after `[Px]` strip and whitespace trim) for two findings to merge. Different LLMs rarely emit byte-identical titles for the same defect — they paraphrase ("Missing null check in handler" vs. "Null pointer not guarded in handler" vs. "Handler dereferences possibly-null variable"). In practice this means cross-reviewer dedup will trigger less often than a fuzzy-matching predicate would, which limits how often `raised_by` populates and how often the confidence-driven primary-body selection fires. **This is a known v1 limitation, accepted as a tradeoff for determinism, Bash 3.2 + jq feasibility (no LLM call, no embedding model), and testability** — the predicate is a pure shell function with pinned regex behavior, fully covered by R6's GWT fixture set. **Mitigation against false-merge risk:** the predicate is conservative on purpose — beyond exact title match, it ALSO requires `priority` equality, exact `file_path` equality, AND non-null overlapping integer line ranges; even if titles fuzzy-matched perfectly, the file+line constraints would still block merges across truly distinct findings. Low hit rate is the acceptable failure mode (two reviewers' findings on the same defect surface as two distinct entries in `aggregate.json` rather than one merged entry); false-merge risk (two distinct defects collapsed into one) is the unacceptable mode and is what the conservative predicate prevents. **v2 deferral:** if telemetry shows the dedup hit rate is meaningfully below user expectations, v2 may explore fuzzier title matching (e.g., normalized token-set similarity, embedding-cosine over titles) gated behind a flag — but only after v1 establishes a hit-rate baseline against which a fuzzier predicate's incremental value can be measured.
- **Risk: malicious-compliance tier disagreement** (someone argues this is M not L). Recorded in Decisions Made with the explicit complexity score; the tier-disagreement protocol is the rebuttal path.

Assert two conjunctive clauses (both required — the test FAILS if either fails, and the two-clause form is deliberately not a single ∀-quantified statement that could be vacuously satisfied when no Round-1 P1 exists at the planted location): **Clause 1 (existence in Round 1):** with `--debate` set and ≥2 reviewers, at least one reviewer MUST raise a P1 finding F at the planted defect's location in Round 1 — i.e., F exists in some reviewer's Round-1 output with `F.priority == "P1"`, non-null `F.file_path` exactly matching the planted defect's `file_path`, and non-null `F.line_start`/`F.line_end` whose integer range overlaps the planted defect's range per R6's overlap predicate. **Clause 2 (retention in final-round aggregate):** that same defect MUST be retained as F' in `aggregate.json` of the final peer round, where "retained" means there exists a finding F' in the final `aggregate.json.findings[]` such that (i) `F'.priority == "P1"`, (ii) `F'.confidence >= 0.7`, (iii) `F'.file_path == F.file_path` (exact match, both non-null), and (iv) `F'.line_start`/`F'.line_end` are non-null and `[F'.line_start, F'.line_end]` overlaps `[F.line_start, F.line_end]` per R6's integer line-range overlap predicate.
