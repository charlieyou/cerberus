Strategy: falsification-first.
Treat this artifact as plausibly wrong and read it like a reviewer who is trying to break it.
Look for concrete counterexamples — inputs, edge cases, configurations, or interleavings — that would make the artifact's claims fail, and where you find one, demonstrate it with evidence: a specific case, an input that triggers it, a citation to where the gap exists.
Reserve your highest-priority findings for counterexamples you can actually exhibit with evidence; adversarial speculation without a demonstrable case is uninformative and clutters the debate.
A finding that names a concrete failing case beats five findings that gesture at "this could fail somehow".
